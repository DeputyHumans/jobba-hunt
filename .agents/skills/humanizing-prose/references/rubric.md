# Scoring rubric

Eight dimensions, each 1–5. Score every variant in Phase 6. Be honest — inflated scores defeat the purpose.

- **Minimum publishable: 28/40**
- **Strong executive content: 34+/40**
- **Below 24: rewrite or delete** — do not ship as-is

When a variant scores below 28, name the specific dimension(s) dragging it down. The user can then decide whether to invest in the missing dimension (usually by supplying more substance) or accept a different variant.

## Dimensions

### 1. Specificity (1 = generic claims · 5 = concrete examples and context)

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | "AI improves productivity." |
| 3 | "AI improves productivity in finance teams." |
| 5 | "AI cut weekly variance reporting from 6 hours to 40 minutes by drafting the first analysis and flagging anomalies before the finance lead opened Excel." |

### 2. Voice (1 = could be anyone · 5 = sounds like a real, specific author)

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Generic LinkedIn-thought-leader register. |
| 3 | Recognizable persona but no distinctive moves. |
| 5 | Voice profile is consistent throughout: matched sentence rhythm, vocabulary register, attitude, and characteristic phrases. A reader familiar with the author would recognize the writing. |

### 3. Judgment (1 = neutral summary · 5 = clear, defensible point of view)

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | "There are pros and cons to AI adoption." |
| 3 | "AI adoption works better when paired with workflow redesign." |
| 5 | "Most companies don't have an AI problem. They have a workflow problem. AI helps only when attached to a decision, a process, and an owner." |

### 4. Usefulness (1 = vague advice · 5 = actionable next steps)

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | "Companies should consider implementing AI strategically." |
| 3 | "Companies should pilot AI in one workflow before scaling." |
| 5 | "Pick one expensive bottleneck — sales research, support triage, invoice review. Measure current cycle time. Use AI to remove the dullest, slowest part. Re-measure after 30 days." |

### 5. Rhythm (1 = same sentence pattern · 5 = natural variation)

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Every sentence within ±20% of the same length; mechanical connectors throughout. |
| 3 | Some variation but recognizable patterns recur (e.g., consistent two-clause structure). |
| 5 | Burstiness rule satisfied throughout; deliberate fragments where they earn it; transitions carry logic; opener and closer do work. |

### 6. Trust (1 = unsupported claims · 5 = verified claims and honest caveats)

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Strong claims with no support; unsourced statistics; implied authority. |
| 3 | Claims with light support; some hedging where appropriate. |
| 5 | Every factual claim is supported, sourced, or honestly hedged. Caveats appear where they should. No invented experience. |

### 7. Reader fit (1 = general audience mush · 5 = tailored to reader's actual needs)

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Generic "business audience" language. |
| 3 | Audience identified but language not fully calibrated. |
| 5 | Vocabulary, examples, and reference points match the named audience. A CEO reads what a CEO needs; an engineer reads what an engineer needs. |

### 8. Originality (1 = repeats common ideas · 5 = adds insight or fresh framing)

| Score | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Restates conventional wisdom. |
| 3 | Conventional wisdom with one fresh angle. |
| 5 | A claim or framing the reader hasn't seen before, defended with evidence. Or a familiar claim defended with original evidence. |

## Scoring procedure

1. Read the variant once at full speed.
2. Score each dimension in turn, not all at once. Resist the temptation to give the same number across the board.
3. Sum and gate. Anything ≥28 is shippable; ≥34 is strong.
4. For variants <28, identify the 1–2 dimensions dragging the score and report them honestly.

## Worked example

Variant text:
> *"Most companies don't have an AI problem. They have a workflow problem. AI helps only when it's attached to a decision, a process, and an owner. Otherwise it becomes another dashboard nobody checks after launch week.*
>
> *Start smaller. Pick one expensive bottleneck: sales research, support triage, invoice review, compliance summaries, forecast variance analysis. Measure current cycle time. Use AI to remove the dullest, slowest, most repeatable part.*
>
> *That's not as glamorous as 'AI transformation.' It's also much harder to fake."*

| Dimension | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Specificity | 4 | Names five concrete bottleneck types; uses "cycle time" and "launch week" as operational terms. Loses one point for not naming a specific company or measured outcome. |
| Voice | 4 | Consistent skeptical-practical register; characteristic moves like "much harder to fake." |
| Judgment | 5 | Clear thesis from the first sentence; defended through the piece. |
| Usefulness | 5 | Concrete instructions: pick, measure, use, re-measure. |
| Rhythm | 4 | Burstiness rule satisfied; opener does work; the "Start smaller." fragment earns its place. |
| Trust | 4 | No invented numbers or false authority. Could improve with one sourced data point. |
| Reader fit | 4 | Calibrated to operations/strategy audience but not named explicitly. |
| Originality | 4 | Familiar claim ("workflow over tool") defended with fresher framing ("dashboard nobody checks after launch week"). |

**Total: 34/40 — strong executive content.**

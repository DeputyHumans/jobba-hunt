I am working on this problem since three years, and my opinion changed two times. At the beginning I
believed the model is most important. After the first deployment in our hospital in Pune, I understood
the data is more important than the model. The nurses did not trust the predictions because the input
was always late by one shift.

So now I think different. The technical part is maybe twenty percent. The rest is who enters the data,
when, and whether they believe the output is for helping them or for checking them. This is not a
machine learning problem. It is a trust problem with a machine learning component.

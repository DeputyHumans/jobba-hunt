# Structural templates

Reader-first structures replace the AI default of `intro → importance → benefits → challenges →
conclusion` symmetric blocks. Pick one based on the piece's purpose, and apply it in the structural
layer of the rewrite. Restraint still applies: if the draft's structure already works, don't impose a
template for its own sake.

## The four templates

### Problem-first
Best for consulting, strategy, B2B essays.
1. Name the real problem (concrete, not abstract).
2. Explain why common thinking about it is wrong.
3. Show what actually works.
4. Give a practical next step.

Example opener: *"Most AI pilots don't fail because the model is weak. They fail because nobody changed
the workflow around the model."*

### Claim-first
Best for executive writing, opinion pieces, LinkedIn-style posts.
1. Strong thesis stated immediately.
2. Evidence supporting it.
3. Implication of the thesis.
4. Counterpoint or concession.
5. Takeaway.

Example opener: *"AI strategy is not a technology plan. It is a decision-rights redesign."*

### Story-first
Best for personal essays, thought leadership, case studies, founder narratives.
1. Situation — set the scene specifically. 2. Friction — what went wrong. 3. Decision — what was chosen,
by whom. 4. Result — what happened. 5. Lesson — what generalizes.
(Only with USER_FACT stake — the situation must be the user's real one, never invented.)

### Inverted pyramid
Best for web content, docs, memos, product pages — anything readers scan.
1. Main answer first. 2. Important details. 3. Supporting context. 4. Edge cases.

## Examples-before-explanations inversion
A structural move available within any template. AI default: define a concept, then give an example.
Human inversion: lead with the concrete example, then name the concept — the abstract term becomes the
natural label for an experience the reader just had. Apply to at least one paragraph when restructuring.

## Opener taxonomy (a working first line is one of these)
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Contrarian | "Your AI roadmap is probably too technical." |
| Practical | "Start with the workflow, not the model." |
| Diagnostic | "If adoption drops after week two, you don't have a training problem. You have a relevance problem." |
| Story | "The client wanted a chatbot. The real problem was their pricing data." |
| Warning | "AI will not fix unclear ownership. It will expose it faster." |

Replace ornamental openers: *"In today's rapidly evolving business landscape…"*, *"As AI continues to
reshape industries…"*, *"The world of [X] is undergoing a transformation…"*.

## Ending taxonomy (a working ending does one of these)
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Next action | "Pick one workflow, define the decision it should improve, assign an owner, and measure whether the work actually gets faster." |
| Sharpen the claim | "AI does not create accountability. It rewards the teams that already have it." |
| Memorable distinction | "The model was accurate. The team still ignored it. That is where most AI programs break: not at prediction, but at adoption." |

Replace ceremonial endings: *"In conclusion, AI offers transformative opportunities…"*, *"Ultimately, the
future of [X] depends on…"*, *"By embracing these strategies, organizations can…"*.

## Passage-priority weighting
Three passages carry the highest **generic-default density** and earn the heaviest edit effort:
**opener** (sets perceived authorship in 1–2 sentences), **transitions** between sections (mechanical
connectors cluster here), **conclusion** (summative clichés cluster here). When budget is tight, fix these
three first — a working opener, logic-bearing transitions, and a non-ceremonial ending carry most of the lift.

## Choosing the template
| Purpose | Use |
|---|---|
| Diagnose and prescribe a fix | Problem-first |
| Stake out a position and defend it | Claim-first |
| Share an experience that generalizes | Story-first |
| Inform a scanner who may not finish | Inverted pyramid |

When unspecified, infer from the draft's purpose; if unclear, ask alongside the voice-profile question.
